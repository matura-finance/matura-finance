The Matura app lives at [`app.usematura.xyz`](https://app.usematura.xyz). You connect a crypto wallet
(on the BNB Chain test network), and from then on everything is organized into a handful of pages you
switch between from the top navigation bar. Matura never touches your wallet's secret key — when an
action needs a signature, your own wallet asks you to approve it.

Here's what each page in the navbar is for.

## Account

Your home base. It shows your balances and the income claims you own, along with where each one
stands — newly created, being financed, or paid out. Think of it as your portfolio view.

{/* screenshot: Account page — drop image at /screens/account.png and embed with ![Account](/screens/account.png) */}

## Get liquidity

The main event. You pick a claim — money you're owed but haven't received yet — and Matura finds you
the **best deal** to get cash for it today. Behind the scenes, several lenders ("vaults") compete, and
Matura shows you the cheapest combination and exactly how much you'll receive. You review it, your
wallet signs, you submit, and the cash is yours. (In the app's navbar this page is labelled
**Get liquidity**.)

{/* screenshot: Get liquidity / request flow — /screens/get-liquidity.png */}

## Activity

Your history. Every financing request and every repayment shows up here in order, each with a link to
the public blockchain record (BscScan) so anyone can verify it actually happened.

{/* screenshot: Activity page — /screens/activity.png */}

## Vaults

The lenders' side. Each vault is a pool of money with its own rules and prices. This page shows what
each vault offers — the choices Matura's best-deal engine picks between on your behalf.

{/* screenshot: Vaults page — /screens/vaults.png */}

## Issuer

A demo tool. In the real world, an employer or platform would vouch for your income by signing a
digital attestation. For this testnet demo, the Issuer page lets you play that role yourself so you can
try the whole flow end to end.
